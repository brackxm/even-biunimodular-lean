# Even biunimodular functions at prime 11

Lean formalization of even biunimodular functions on the cyclic group of
order 11. These are complex-valued functions satisfying `f(-x) = f(x)`,
with both the function and its unitary Fourier transform having modulus one.
Functions are normalized by `f(0) = 1`.
All project Lean sources and exact certificate data are in `lean/`.

Proved results include:

- Exactly 80 normalized even biunimodular functions: 10 quadratic Gaussian
  and 70 non-Gaussian.
- The fixed slice with 32 complex and 16 real projective points, its quotient
  spectrum, and rank-two Gram moduli.
- Conductor-77 arithmetic and the S8 Galois group of the degree-eight
  invariant factor.
- Seven distinct projective Fourier/Weyl symmetry orbits generated by the
  non-Gaussian even solutions, each containing 1210 projective points.
- The reduced 252-point complex scheme of the even phase equations, with its
  exact eliminant and rational univariate representation (RUR).

With [elan](https://github.com/leanprover/elan) installed, run from this directory:

```sh
cd lean
lake exe cache get
lake --wfail build
```

`lean/lean-toolchain` pins Lean 4.34.1; `lean/lake-manifest.json` pins the dependencies.
[Biunimodular.lean](lean/Biunimodular.lean) is the main entry point. The default
build also checks the separate [complex scheme](lean/Biunimodular/F11/ComplexSchemeResults.lean),
[character cutout](lean/Biunimodular/F11/CharacterCutoutResults.lean), and
[RUR parametrization](lean/Biunimodular/F11/RurResults.lean) endpoints and the
axiom audits. All exact certificates are included; building requires no
external certificate data or generator scripts. Large certificates use `native_decide`;
the trust boundary includes Lean's native execution mechanism.

Public summaries use the `Biunimodular.F11.Results` namespace; object-level
definitions and lemmas use `Biunimodular.F11`.

AI coding agents assisted the development of this formalization and its
certificates. AI output is not itself evidence of correctness; formal claims
rely on Lean verification, including the native execution trust boundary.

## References

- Göran Björck, [*Functions of modulus 1 on Z_n whose Fourier transforms have
  constant modulus, and “cyclic n-roots”*](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-94-009-0665-5_10).
  In *Recent Advances in Fourier Analysis and Its Applications*, NATO ASI Series
  C 315, pp. 131–140 (1990). Background on cyclic roots and the square-class
  sequences excluded by the even classification.
- Göran Björck and Uffe Haagerup, [*All cyclic p-roots of index 3, found by
  symmetry-preserving calculations*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0803.2506) (2008).
  A precedent for the rank-two matrix and cofactor methods used in the Gram
  description.
- Uffe Haagerup, [*Cyclic p-roots of prime lengths p and related complex Hadamard
  matrices*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0803.2629) (2008). Section 7 gives the
  simple-index count with multiplicity; index 5 gives `binomial(10, 5) = 252`,
  the count relevant to the even complex scheme at prime 11.
- Hartmut Führ and Ziemowit Rzeszotnik, [*On biunimodular vectors for unitary
  matrices*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.06738). *Linear Algebra and its
  Applications* **484**, pp. 86–129 (2015),
  [doi:10.1016/j.laa.2015.06.019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2015.06.019).
  Background on Fourier symmetries, including the numerical observation of
  seven 1210-point orbits at prime 11.
- Lawrence C. Washington, [*Introduction to Cyclotomic Fields*](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-1934-7),
  2nd edition, Graduate Texts in Mathematics 83, Springer (1997). Background for
  the cyclotomic fields and character arithmetic used at conductor 77.
